Not so intelligent

December 2025 7 min read
LLMs Generative AI Explainability

Your favorite AI assistant isn't intelligent.

Yes. You read that right. And that's not a bad thing.

As a user, understanding how these AI tools work is actually very important. Why? Because the faster you understand this, the better you will be at using them.

These tools are not smart in the human sense. They just have a huge amount of training data and a lot of computing power to process things very fast.

A small experiment

Read the sentence below and fill in the blank:

Twinkle Twinkle Little ___

Did you think of Star? Most people do. But pause for a second. How did you get there? Did you consciously analyze grammar rules? Did you logically reason it out?

Probably not. You have just seen this pattern before. Many times.

That is exactly how large language models — the systems behind popular AI assistants — work. They do not understand meaning. They recognize patterns.

How language models actually work

It starts with training. Language models are trained on an enormous amount of text: books, articles, websites, conversations, code, and more. Much more than any human could ever read.

From this text, the model learns patterns like:

  • which words tend to appear together
  • what usually comes next in a sentence
  • how language typically flows

That is all. No understanding. Just patterns.

Once training is complete, the model is released for people to use. The moment you type something — your prompt — the model starts gathering context, just like you did when you saw "Twinkle Twinkle little." Then it starts generating a response one word at a time:

  • it looks at the context so far
  • predicts the most likely next word
  • adds it
  • repeats

You are not talking to a thinking being. You are watching a very fast sentence-completion system at work.

Why does it feel so intelligent?

Two main reasons.

First, scale. These models have seen more text than any human ever could.

Second, speed. They run on powerful hardware — GPUs — which allows them to process large amounts of context in milliseconds. That is why they sound fluent, adapt to your tone, explain things smoothly, and appear to reason. But underneath, they are still predicting what comes next.

How to use AI tools well

Once you understand this, the relationship becomes clear.

You do the thinking. The AI does the typing.

The model builds its response based on your input. So vague input leads to vague output. You are not really asking a question — you are setting up a sentence for the model to complete. So be clear. Give context. Explain what you want.

You are the problem solver. The AI is the tool that helps you move faster.

  • Marketer? You set the strategy. It drafts the copy.
  • Engineer? You design the system. It helps write the code.
  • Writer? You decide the plot. It helps refine tone and flow.

Never expect these tools to invent something truly original on their own. They remix what already exists very well, but you still decide direction and creativity.

A quick safety note

These models hallucinate. Sometimes they sound confident but are wrong. They lose track of earlier context. They generate sentences that do not fully make sense.

This happens because they are not checking facts — they are predicting patterns. That is why cross-verifying matters.

Use AI tools to brainstorm, draft, and explore ideas. But always keep humans in the loop for correctness, judgment, and final decisions.

One last thing to remember

AI language models do not understand language. They predict it. Once you truly get that, everything else about these tools makes sense.
